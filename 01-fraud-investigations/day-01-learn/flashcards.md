# Week 1 — Day 1 Flashcards

## Fraud Investigation Fundamentals

| # | Front | Back |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is predication? | A reasonable factual basis that justifies opening or pursuing an investigation. |
| 2 | What is a red flag? | An indicator that activity may be suspicious or unusual and warrants further examination. |
| 3 | Does a red flag prove fraud? | No. A red flag identifies something to investigate; it does not determine the conclusion. |
| 4 | What is first-party fraud? | Fraud involving a person's own identity, account, or relationship with an organization while engaging in deceptive behavior. |
| 5 | What is third-party fraud? | Unauthorized use of another person's identity, account, credentials, or information. |
| 6 | What is account takeover? | Unauthorized control or access to an existing account. |
| 7 | What is occupational fraud? | Fraud committed by an employee against the organization where they work. |
| 8 | What is a fraud typology? | A recognizable method or pattern used to commit fraud. |
| 9 | What is triage? | Prioritizing an allegation or alert and determining the appropriate handling. |
| 10 | What is a hypothesis? | A proposed explanation that can be tested against evidence. |
| 11 | Why use competing hypotheses? | To reduce confirmation bias and test multiple plausible explanations against the evidence. |
| 12 | What is an evidence gap? | Important information that is currently unavailable but is needed to answer an investigative question. |
| 13 | Does account or system access prove responsibility? | No. Access is relevant evidence but does not by itself establish responsibility. |
| 14 | What is disposition? | The final handling or conclusion of a case. |
| 15 | What does inconclusive mean? | Available evidence is insufficient to determine what happened or whether the allegation is supported. |
| 16 | What is substantiation? | A determination that available evidence sufficiently supports an allegation. |
| 17 | What is the fraud investigation lifecycle? | Alert → Intake → Triage → Predication → Hypothesis → Evidence → Analysis → Findings → Disposition. |
| 18 | What is the investigator's core principle? | Follow the evidence, not the suspicion. |

## Applied Framework

**Red flag → Investigative question → Evidence → Analysis → Finding → Disposition**

Example:

- **Red flag:** Four high-value purchases in California within 45 minutes, inconsistent with historical activity.
- **Question:** Did the customer authorize the transactions?
- **Evidence:** Customer location, transaction history, IP information, device/authentication records, customer statement.
