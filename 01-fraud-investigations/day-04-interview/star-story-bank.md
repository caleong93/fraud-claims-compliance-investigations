# Week 1 STAR Story Bank

## STAR Story #1 — Organized Retail Crime Investigation
### Situation
An organized retail crime group was traveling throughout Virginia, and the company was having difficulty developing enough evidence to catch the group and build a case.

### Task
I needed to understand the group's method of operation, identify its pattern, coordinate with other investigators, and develop sufficient evidence for law enforcement.

### Action
I watched the group at least twice live on camera to understand its MO. Once I identified the pattern, I partnered with a sister-store detective and our ORC investigator. We created a heat map and coordinated coverage around the times the group was expected to come into the stores. We developed a plan to capture the necessary evidence on camera and coordinated with police so they could be positioned outside the store. We then documented the activity across the five steps of retail theft.

### Result
We apprehended all five individuals, charges were filed, and we were able to connect prior theft incidents to build a broader case that could be taken to court.

### Key Competencies
Investigation; pattern recognition; evidence gathering; collaboration; planning; persistence; law-enforcement coordination; case development.

## STAR Story #2 — Evidence and Analysis: Markdown Investigation
### Situation
Two internal employees who were part of the markdown team were reported for allegedly ticket switching while performing markdowns.

### Task
I needed to determine whether the activity was legitimate markdown activity or intentional ticket manipulation.

### Action
I collected the markdown lists, POS transactions, camera footage, and conducted live review. I used those sources together to compare the employees' activity and validate whether the markdowns were being performed correctly. I found some discrepancies and observed that the employees were purchasing items at unusually low prices. However, despite those indicators, I could not obtain direct evidence showing that they were intentionally ticket switching.

### Result
I documented the case as undetermined because the available evidence did not substantiate the allegation. I did not reach a conclusion simply because the activity looked suspicious.

### Strong Interview Line
"Although there were indicators that raised suspicion, I couldn't substantiate the allegation with the available evidence, so I documented the case as undetermined rather than reaching a conclusion that the evidence didn't support."

### Key Competencies
Evidence analysis; objectivity; corroboration; investigative judgment; documentation; appropriate disposition.

## STAR Story #3 — Conflicting Information: Gift Card Investigation
### Situation
A manager believed a cashier was adding gift cards to customer transactions and then stealing the gift cards.

### Task
I needed to determine whether the activity was an innocent transaction issue, such as a customer changing their mind, or intentional theft.

### Action
I reviewed the cashier's transactions involving gift cards and found multiple transactions where the gift card was voided. That created two competing possibilities: the customer could have changed their mind, or the cashier could have intended to steal the gift card but not followed through. I reviewed surveillance to corroborate the transaction activity. The initial footage appeared to show what could have been a mistake, so I did not treat the transaction pattern alone as sufficient evidence. I then coordinated a controlled observation in which management stayed away from the registers, allowing the cashier to perform transactions without knowing the activity was being specifically observed. During that observation, I saw the cashier add a gift card to the purchase and place the gift card in their pocket.

### Result
The direct observation corroborated the earlier transaction concerns and established intentional behavior that could be supported with evidence.

### Strong Interview Framing
Avoid: "I knew she was attempting to steal."

Use: "The transaction pattern and behavior increased my suspicion, but I didn't consider that sufficient evidence by itself. I needed to observe the behavior and obtain evidence that directly corroborated the concern."

### Key Competencies
Conflicting information; evidence development; behavioral observation; corroboration; investigative judgment.
